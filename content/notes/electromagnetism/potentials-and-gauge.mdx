title: Potentials and Gauge Freedom
summary: Trading six field components for four potentials, and the redundancy that comes with them.

Maxwell's equations are six coupled equations for six field components. Two of them,
the ones with no sources, are constraints rather than dynamics: they say the magnetic
field has no divergence and the electric field's curl is tied to a changing magnetic
field. Those two can be solved once and for all by writing the fields in terms of
potentials, which is both a simplification and, as it turns out, a source of a subtle kind
of freedom.

## Solving two equations for free

Since $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ everywhere, $\mathbf{B}$ is the curl of something:

$$
\mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}.
$$

Put that into Faraday's law and it forces $\nabla \times (\mathbf{E} + \partial_t
\mathbf{A}) = 0$, so the combination in the bracket is a gradient:

$$
\mathbf{E} = -\nabla\varphi - \frac{\partial \mathbf{A}}{\partial t}.
$$

Four numbers now, the scalar $\varphi$ and the vector $\mathbf{A}$, in place of six, and
the two source-free Maxwell equations are satisfied automatically by construction.

## The freedom

The potentials are not unique. The same fields come from

$$
\mathbf{A} \to \mathbf{A} + \nabla\lambda, \qquad
\varphi \to \varphi - \frac{\partial \lambda}{\partial t},
$$

for any scalar function $\lambda(\mathbf{r},t)$, because the curl of a gradient and the
mismatched time derivative cancel exactly. This is a gauge transformation, and the fact
that no measurement can tell the two sets of potentials apart is gauge freedom.

<Statement kind="Definition" title="Fixing a gauge">
Choosing a condition that pins down $\lambda$ is called fixing the gauge. It removes the
redundancy without changing any field. The two common choices are the Coulomb gauge,
$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A}=0$, and the Lorenz gauge, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} +
\mu_0\varepsilon_0\,\partial_t\varphi = 0$.
</Statement>

## Why the Lorenz gauge is worth it

Substitute the potentials into the two Maxwell equations that do have sources, and in the
Lorenz gauge the mess uncouples into two identical wave equations:

$$
\Box\,\varphi = -\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}, \qquad
\Box\,\mathbf{A} = -\mu_0\,\mathbf{J},
$$

where $\Box = \nabla^2 - \frac{1}{c^2}\partial_t^2$. Each potential obeys a wave equation
driven by its own source, the charge for $\varphi$ and the current for $\mathbf{A}$.
Nothing could be cleaner, and the solutions are the retarded potentials, fields that
arrive at a distance a time $r/c$ after the source moved.

<Aside>
The Coulomb gauge makes $\varphi$ the instantaneous Coulomb potential of the charge, as if
signals travelled infinitely fast. That is not a paradox: $\varphi$ is not measurable on
its own, and the acausal-looking part cancels against $\mathbf{A}$ when you form
$\mathbf{E}$. The freedom to shuffle where the retardation sits is the gauge freedom
again.
</Aside>

The redundancy is not a defect to be tidied away and forgotten. Insisting that physics
should not depend on the choice of $\lambda$ is, in more advanced treatments, exactly what
forces the existence of the electromagnetic field in the first place. Here it is enough to
notice that the freedom is real, that fixing it is a convenience rather than a physical
statement, and that the right fix turns six coupled equations into two wave equations.
