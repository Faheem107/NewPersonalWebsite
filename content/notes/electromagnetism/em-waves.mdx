Maxwell's contribution was a single term. Ampère's law as inherited was
$\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{J}$, which is inconsistent with charge
conservation. Take the divergence and you get $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$, which is
false whenever charge accumulates anywhere. Adding the displacement current fixes it:

$$
\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0\varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}
$$

## Light, unasked for

In vacuum, take the curl of Faraday's law and substitute:

$$
\nabla^2 \mathbf{E} = \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial^2 \mathbf{E}}{\partial t^2}
$$

A wave equation, with speed

$$
c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}}
$$

Both constants came from benchtop experiments on capacitors and current-carrying
wires. Neither had anything to do with optics. The number that came out was the
measured speed of light, and there was no longer any question about what light was.

## Polarisation and energy

The fields are transverse and mutually perpendicular, with
$\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{B}$, $\hat{\mathbf{k}}$ forming a right-handed set and
$|\mathbf{E}| = c|\mathbf{B}|$. Energy flux is the Poynting vector:

$$
\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}
$$

<Aside>
$\mathbf{S}$ also carries momentum density $\mathbf{S}/c^2$. Light pushes. It is a
small push, sunlight delivers about $9\ \mu\mathrm{N}$ per square metre, but it is
enough to sail on, and enough to matter inside a star.
</Aside>

## Dispersion

In a medium the speed becomes $c/n$, and $n$ depends on frequency because the bound
electrons are driven oscillators with their own resonances. Model them as damped
harmonic oscillators and you get

$$
n^2(\omega) = 1 + \frac{Ne^2}{\varepsilon_0 m}\sum_j \frac{f_j}{\omega_j^2 - \omega^2 - i\gamma_j\omega}
$$

Away from resonance $n$ rises with frequency, so blue refracts more than red. Prisms,
rainbows and chromatic aberration are all this formula.

Near resonance the medium absorbs strongly and $n$ can fall below one, so the phase
velocity exceeds $c$. Nothing is violated: information travels at the group velocity,
which does not.

## Boundaries

Matching $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ across an interface gives the Fresnel
coefficients, and with them essentially all of practical optics.

<Statement kind="Result" title="Brewster's angle">
At $\theta_B = \arctan(n_2/n_1)$ the reflected wave is completely polarised. The
dipoles in the medium oscillate along the direction the reflected ray would have to
travel, and a dipole radiates nothing along its own axis. Polarising sunglasses cut
glare for exactly this reason: light reflected off water and roads is horizontally
polarised.
</Statement>

Past the critical angle for total internal reflection the transmitted wave does not
vanish; it becomes evanescent, decaying exponentially into the second medium while
carrying no net energy across. Bring a third surface close enough and it tunnels,
the optical analogue of quantum tunnelling, and the mechanism behind fibre couplers.

## Radiation needs acceleration

A static charge has a field but radiates nothing. A charge in uniform motion is a static
charge in another frame, so it radiates nothing either. Only acceleration radiates, and
the power follows Larmor's formula:

$$
P = \frac{q^2 a^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3}
$$

<Statement kind="Consequence" title="Why classical atoms cannot exist">
An electron orbiting a proton is accelerating, so it must radiate. Feeding the hydrogen
ground state into Larmor gives a spiral into the nucleus in about $1.6 \times 10^{-11}$
seconds. Classical electromagnetism does not merely fail to explain atomic stability. It
positively forbids it, by eleven orders of magnitude. Every atom around you is a standing
refutation, and resolving it required abandoning the orbit entirely.
</Statement>

Dipole radiation goes as $\sin^2\theta$, with nothing emitted along the dipole axis.
That single angular factor explains an unreasonable amount: why the sky is polarised at
ninety degrees to the Sun, why Brewster's angle exists at all, and why an antenna is
oriented the way it is.

## Why the sky is blue, quantitatively

Scattering off particles much smaller than the wavelength scales as $\omega^4$. Blue light
at 450 nm scatters roughly $(700/450)^4 \approx 5.9$ times more strongly than red at
700 nm.

It is the same $\omega^4$ that appears in the dispersion formula above: a driven
oscillator far below resonance, radiating according to Larmor. Blue sky, red sunsets and
the polarisation of both follow from that one calculation.
